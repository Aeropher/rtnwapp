RightNow is built on one idea: a day only holds so much. Your capacity is the time you have for tasks today. You plan up to it, not past it.

## Why capacity

Most to-do lists don't care how long things take. You can put twelve hours of work on a list for an eight-hour day and it looks fine until the evening, when half of it is still there.

RightNow adds up how long your tasks take and compares that with the time you have. If the day is full, you find out while you're planning, not at six o'clock. The point isn't to cram more in. It's to commit to what fits, get it done, and stop.

That only works if your tasks have honest durations. A task left at the default 30 minutes that really takes two hours makes the whole day look lighter than it is.

## How your capacity is worked out

Both settings live in **Settings > Planning > Daily Capacity**.

- **Working hours** sets when each day of the week starts and finishes, or marks it as **Not a working day**. On a working day, the time you have is the time left until your working hours finish. At 2pm with a 5:30pm finish, that's three and a half hours, whatever you had planned that morning.
- **Default Daily Capacity** is a fixed number of minutes. RightNow uses it on days that aren't working days. Edit it there, or tap one of the **Capacity Presets**.

Be realistic about what counts. If you're at work for eight hours but spend two in meetings and one on email, you have nearer five hours for tasks. Connect your calendar and the meetings can be taken off for you (see below).

## Reading the capacity bar

The bar at the top of **Today** fills up as you plan.

- Each block is one task, sized by how long it takes, in the same order as your Today list.
- Calendar events set to count toward capacity show in blue.
- Anything that runs past your capacity is red. That's the part of your plan that won't fit today.

Tap the bar to switch what the text beside it says: when your day ends, how much time you have left, or how long until midnight.

When you plan a task for today from the **Inbox** and it won't fit, RightNow tells you. You can still add it. It's your day.

## Capacity and time blocking

Time blocking means giving every task its own slot in your calendar: email at 9, the report from 10 until 12. It works well until one thing overruns. Then everything after it is wrong, and rebuilding the plan takes time you don't have.

Capacity is a looser version of the same idea. You still budget your hours, but you don't pin each task to a time. The order of your Today list is the plan, and the bar tells you whether it fits. When something overruns, the bar shows what has fallen off the end, and you move a task to tomorrow instead of redrawing the whole day.

You can still block time where it helps:

- **Take meetings out of your day.** Connect Google Calendar in **Settings > Calendar**. On a meeting you want counted, turn on **Counts toward capacity** with the switch on the event in Today, or open the event and turn it on there. All-day events never count. Its time comes off your capacity and shows in blue on the bar.
- **Give a task a fixed time.** In the task editor, under **When**, tap the time row (it says **Anytime** until you set one) and pick a time.
- **See those tasks in your calendar.** Turn on **Show tasks in Google Calendar** in **Settings > Calendar**. Every task with a time appears in a calendar called "RightNow", in a slot as long as the task takes. It only works one way: change the task in RightNow, because edits made in Google Calendar are overwritten.

A good mix for most people is to block the things that really are fixed, like meetings, appointments and the school run, and leave everything else as a list that fits in the time that's left.
